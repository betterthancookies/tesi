\section*{Summary}

\begin{itemize}
  \item New scenario requested by the supervisor: \textbf{ResNet-18 on
        CIFAR-100, 100 clients, 20 per round}; baselines at $E=5$, $B=128$,
        SAGE-smart with $E\in[2,10]$ and $B\in\{16,\dots,128\}$. Everything is
        selected by parameters; ResNet-20/CIFAR-10 is still available.
  \item The cost of the \textbf{state broadcast} at the start of each round
        (option 1 of the ``Client Server Protocol'', 10\,\% of the clients do
        not answer) is now charged to SAGE, ESCS and SAGE-smart: about
        \SI{7}{}--\SI{10}{\watt\hour} per run, \textbf{less than 1\,\%} of the
        energy.
  \item Battery capacities are now those of the \textbf{same three phones}
        whose speed and power we already use (\SI{4820}{} / \SI{4780}{} /
        \SI{5000}{\milli\ampere\hour}), and the clients train with the standard
        CIFAR \textbf{data augmentation}. A first campaign without
        augmentation showed that ResNet-18 memorised the training data within
        about 100 rounds (Section~\ref{sec:first}).
  \item Main campaign (33 runs): averaged over the three $\beta$, SAGE-smart
        reaches about the accuracy of FedAvg at $B=128$ (52.5--53.2\,\% vs
        51.1\,\%) with \textbf{36\,\% less energy}, a mean final SoC of 0.39
        instead of 0.14, and \textbf{7--8 depleted devices instead of 66}. The
        result holds in the three battery worlds.
  \item \textbf{Ablation} (24 runs, Section~\ref{sec:ablation}): the
        advantage comes from three components. Adaptive epochs give the energy
        saving and the battery protection; the staleness term and the small
        batch give the accuracy. The server-side SoC estimate, the negotiation
        and FedNova have no measurable effect in this scenario.
  \item \textbf{FedAvg at $B=16$} (3 runs, Section~\ref{sec:b16}), the fair
        reference for the batch: \textbf{58.0\,\%}, 5.5 points above SAGE-smart,
        for the same energy and the same depletions as FedAvg at $B=128$. It
        also reaches the final accuracy of SAGE-smart \textbf{with 19--31\,\%
        less energy} (round 83--100). What SAGE-smart keeps is the protection of
        the batteries over a long run: 8 depleted devices instead of 67, mean
        SoC 0.39 instead of 0.16, 37\,\% less energy over 250 rounds, at the
        price of about 5 accuracy points.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Cost of one participation (ResNet-18, 400 samples, $E=5$).}
Training costs 0.199 / 0.139 / \SI{0.187}{\watt\hour} on the low / mid / high
tier, that is \textbf{1.12 / 0.79 / 1.01\,\%} of the battery (it was 0.3--0.4\,\%
with ResNet-20). One model exchange costs \SI{181}{\joule}
(\SI{0.05}{\watt\hour}, 0.28\,\% of the battery) and \SI{50}{\second}
(\SI{14}{\second} down, \SI{36}{\second} up). The SAGE-smart deadline is about
\SI{130}{\second}.

\paragraph{The broadcast.} At the start of every round SAGE, ESCS and SAGE-smart
ask all alive clients for their state. 10\,\% do not answer (indication of the
supervisor) and are excluded from that round; the server waits
\SI{1}{\second} for them. A client that answers and is not selected pays a full
4G session (\SI{2.1}{\joule}); a selected client pays only the payload, because
the model download follows in the same radio session. Over a run this costs
\SI{7}{}--\SI{10}{\watt\hour} (0.4--0.8\,\% of the total energy) and
\SI{250}{\second} of waiting. In the simulator the broadcast changes no
information (SAGE and ESCS already read the true SoC, SAGE-smart its estimate):
it only makes us pay for the fresh state we assume.

\section{A first campaign without augmentation}
\label{sec:first}

The first ResNet-18 campaign (\texttt{ResNet18\_C100\_bcast}, old capacities,
no augmentation) gave low accuracies (FedAvg 22--28\,\%, SAGE-smart 35--38\,\%)
and very many depleted devices (FedAvg 83--91 out of 100). Looking at the
choices of SAGE-smart revealed why:

\begin{itemize}
  \item the loss of the global model on the clients' own data dropped to
        \textbf{0.002} by round 125, while the test accuracy stayed at 37\,\%:
        the model had \textbf{memorised} the training data (11 million
        parameters, 40\,000 images, no augmentation, no weight decay);
  \item SAGE-smart then chose the minimum work, $E=2$, for every client from
        round 75 on, because its utility (loss$/\ln C$) was zero. Part of its
        energy saving was a consequence of overfitting.
\end{itemize}

For this reason the main campaign uses the standard CIFAR augmentation (He et
al.\ 2016) on the clients' training data; test sets are untouched. At the same
time the capacities were set to those of the three phones.
Table~\ref{tab:before_after} shows the effect of the two changes together.

points; \textbf{the energy saving does not shrink} ($-33\,\%$ $\to$ $-36\,\%$).
So the saving was not an artefact of overfitting.

\paragraph{What the results say.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Per Wh, SAGE-smart beats every algorithm run at $B=128$}
        (Figure~\ref{fig:energy}). To reach 45\,\% accuracy (mean of 10
        rounds) it needs 1044 / 538 / \SI{569}{\watt\hour} at $\beta=0.1$ /
        0.5 / 1.0, against 1374 / 995 / \SI{885}{\watt\hour} for FedAvg
        ($-24$ to $-46\,\%$). 50\,\% is reached by SAGE-smart at 758 / \SI{818}{\watt\hour}
        ($\beta=0.5$ / 1.0) and by FedAvg at 1553 / \SI{1212}{\watt\hour}; SAGE
        and ESCS never reach it. \textbf{It does not beat FedAvg at $B=16$}
        (Section~\ref{sec:b16}).
  \item \textbf{Per round, SAGE-smart starts slower} and overtakes FedAvg only
        after about 180--200 rounds (Figure~\ref{fig:rounds}). It inherits the
        ramp of SAGE ($2, 3, \dots$ clients in the first rounds, 20 from round
        19) and trains 2--4 epochs instead of 5. With a budget of fewer than
        about 180 rounds FedAvg would reach a higher accuracy.
  \item \textbf{Batteries}: FedAvg depletes 66 devices out of 100 and leaves 75
        below \SI{20}{\percent}; SAGE-smart 7--8 and 22--25. The first depletion
        comes at round 57 with FedAvg and at round 159--175 with SAGE-smart.
        The 4--10 depletions of SAGE-smart all come after round 135, never on
        the low tier and mostly on the high tier (\SI{6.15}{\watt} at full
        load), which drains the most between rounds.
  \item \textbf{SAGE and ESCS} protect the batteries better than FedAvg (23--39
        depleted) but lose 3.5--7 accuracy points for the same or slightly more
        energy (1956--\SI{2124}{\watt\hour} vs 1984): they keep more devices
        alive, and every alive device pays the idle between rounds
        (\SI{919}{}--\SI{1081}{\watt\hour} of idle against 806 for FedAvg).
  \item \textbf{Training energy of SAGE-smart is half that of FedAvg}
        (444--462 vs \SI{937}{\watt\hour}) for about the same number of
        participations (communication 216--225 vs \SI{242}{\watt\hour}): it is
        the effect of fewer epochs per participation (next paragraph).
  \item \textbf{The battery world changes little}: for ESCS, the SAGE ablation
        and SAGE-smart, linear, Peukert and datasheet differ by at most 1
        accuracy point and 4\,\% of energy. (SAGE in the linear world is the
        paper's version, a different algorithm from the ablation.)
  \item FedAvg and FedProx consume exactly the same energy: same seed, hence
        same random selection, and same declared work. Only the accuracy
        differs ($\pm0.2$ points).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{SAGE-smart: how many epochs the clients choose.} With
augmentation the loss of the global model on the clients' data no longer
collapses, but SAGE-smart still moves towards the minimum $E$
(Table~\ref{tab:epochs}). Two causes: the deadline caps the envelope at
$E_\text{hi}\approx 3.5$--4 for 19--20 clients out of 20, and the utility
$u=\text{loss}/\ln 100$ falls below 0.3 after round 100, so the client picks
$E_\text{lo}=2$. The choice of $E$ is therefore mostly a consequence of the
deadline, computed on the baselines' nominal work ($E=5$, $B=128$).

\section{Ablation: which part of SAGE-smart brings the advantage}
\label{sec:ablation}

Leave-one-out, as proposed in the 12/10 report: full SAGE-smart, then the same
algorithm with \textbf{one} component removed, everything else unchanged.
Datasheet battery, same configuration as the main campaign, three $\beta$, seed
2026, plus FedAvg in the same battery world (24 runs).

\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{staleness}: $c=0$ in the score $a\cdot\text{SoC}+b\cdot D+c\cdot\text{staleness}$;
  \item \emph{SoC estimate}: the server ranks with the last reported SoC,
        without extrapolating the idle consumption since the report;
  \item \emph{negotiation}: no proposal, no accept/refuse, no replacement: the
        training message goes directly to the top of the ranking (the client
        still chooses $(E,B)$ and can still withdraw);
  \item \emph{adaptive epochs}: $E=5$ fixed as in the baselines ($B$ still
        chosen by the client);
  \item \emph{FedNova}: plain data-weighted average (BatchNorm statistics
        averaged as before);
  \item \emph{small batch}: $B=128$ fixed as in the baselines ($E$ still
        chosen by the client).
\end{itemize}

The full SAGE-smart run of the ablation is identical to the one of the main
campaign (bit for bit at $\beta=0.1$ and 0.5, 0.04 accuracy points at
$\beta=1.0$): differences between variants come from the component, not from
the GPU. They still include the variability of a single seed (see below).

        fewer. With $E=5$ fixed SAGE-smart uses as much energy as FedAvg
        (2052 vs \SI{1963}{\watt\hour}) and still depletes 31 devices, against 68
        for FedAvg (that half of the protection comes from the SoC term of the
        score, the reserve and the energy guard, which are still active). The
        $+7.5$ accuracy points must be read with care: to fit 5 epochs in the
        deadline the clients raise the batch to $B\approx 85$--104, and 4--7
        times more participations arrive late. The two effects (more epochs,
        larger batch) are mixed.
  \item \textbf{Small batch: $+6.7$ accuracy points at no cost} (energy
        $\pm\SI{40}{\watt\hour}$, same depletions). With ResNet-18 a batch costs
        about \SI{1}{\second} of computation on the phone and the fixed cost per
        batch (\SI{0.057}{\second}) becomes negligible: a smaller batch gives
        more SGD steps for the same energy. \textbf{Without it, SAGE-smart is
        5 points below FedAvg} (45.7 vs 50.8\,\%): the accuracy advantage over
        FedAvg comes from the batch. FedAvg gains the same from it
        (Section~\ref{sec:b16}).
  \item \textbf{Staleness: $+8.8$ accuracy points}, paid with 6 points of SoC
        and \SI{38}{\watt\hour}. Without it the score keeps choosing the same
        clients: their batteries reach the reserve (598--894 refusals per run
        instead of 12--74), part of the data is almost never used, and the
        server's knowledge of the others ages (SoC error 0.19--0.23 instead of
        0.02--0.06). The higher mean SoC is the energy of the clients that
        never train.
  \item \textbf{SoC estimate, negotiation and FedNova: no measurable effect}
        (on average within 1.2 accuracy points, 1 SoC point, 3 devices and
        \SI{30}{\watt\hour}). This is expected in this scenario:
        \begin{itemize}
          \item rounds are back to back and every candidate reports its state at
                every proposal, so the last report is almost as good as the
                estimate (its error is even slightly lower: 0.015--0.027 against
                0.018--0.056, probably because the estimate does not foresee
                recharges);
          \item refusals are few (12--74 per run); without negotiation 6--82
                clients per run download the model and then withdraw, which
                costs little. The negotiation saves 3 and 6 devices at
                $\beta=0.1$ and 0.5, where refusals exist;
          \item plain averaging works as well as FedNova with a step spread of
                10--12.
        \end{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Variability of one seed.} At $\beta=1.0$ three different
        variants (no estimate, no negotiation, no FedNova) all end 2.7--3.1
        points above full SAGE-smart. Most likely the full run at $\beta=1.0$
        is below its average by chance: it has a transient collapse in the first
        rounds (accuracy back to 1\,\% after round 1, with 2 clients and a step
        spread of 14). Differences of up to about 3 points should not be read
        as effects without more seeds.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{In one sentence.} The energy and battery advantage of SAGE-smart
comes from letting the clients train fewer epochs (adaptive $E$, bounded by
the deadline); its accuracy comes from the small batch and from the staleness
term, which spreads participation over all clients. The other three components
are neutral here. Since FedAvg gains as much from the small batch, only the
first part (energy and batteries) is an advantage over a well-tuned FedAvg
(Section~\ref{sec:b16}).

\section{FedAvg at $B=16$: the fair reference for the batch}

that without it SAGE-smart is 5 points below FedAvg. The baselines run at
$B=128$; with ResNet-18 a smaller batch costs almost nothing more (the fixed
cost per batch is negligible), so the fair question is how FedAvg does at
$B=16$. Campaign \texttt{sweepB16}: FedAvg at $B=16$, $E=5$, everything else as

  \item \textbf{The batch alone is worth about 7 points to FedAvg}: 51.1 $\to$
        58.0\,\% (+6.3 / +7.0 / +7.2 at $\beta=0.1$ / 0.5 / 1.0) for 1\,\%
        more energy and the same depletions. It confirms the ablation (+6.7
        for SAGE-smart) and the ResNet-20 batch sweep.
  \item \textbf{FedAvg at $B=16$ is 5.5 points more accurate than
        SAGE-smart} and \textbf{more efficient per Wh at every $\beta$}
        (Figure~\ref{fig:b16}, left). It reaches 45\,\% with 781 / 555 /
        \SI{504}{\watt\hour} (SAGE-smart: 1044 / 538 / 569) and the final
        accuracy of SAGE-smart with 19--31\,\% less energy
        (Table~\ref{tab:b16_iso}). The claim ``same accuracy with less
        energy'' holds only against the baselines at $B=128$.
  \item \textbf{What SAGE-smart keeps is the protection of the batteries
        over a long run.} Over 250 rounds FedAvg at $B=16$ depletes 67 devices
        out of 100 and leaves 73 below \SI{20}{\percent}; SAGE-smart 8 and
        25, with a mean SoC of 0.39 instead of 0.16 and 37\,\% less energy.
        Even when FedAvg at $B=16$ has just reached the accuracy of
        SAGE-smart, it has already depleted 21 and 24 devices at $\beta=0.1$
        and 0.5 (7 at $\beta=1.0$, as many as SAGE-smart after 250 rounds).
  \item \textbf{Why SAGE-smart learns less per Wh.} Two design choices, not
        the battery: (i) the deadline is the median nominal time of the
        baselines' work ($E=5$, $B=128$, about \SI{130}{\second}), so by
        construction about half of the clients cannot fit 5 epochs and
        SAGE-smart trains 2--3 epochs even when the battery would allow more;
        (ii) the ramp inherited from SAGE (2 clients in round 1, 20 from round
        19) slows the start (Figure~\ref{fig:b16}, centre). Both can be
        changed: the deadline has a parameter (\texttt{smart\_deadline\_mult}),
        the ramp needs a small switch.
\end{itemize}

%======================================================================
\section{Limits}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{One seed}, as before. The ablation shows that a single run can
        differ by about 3 points from its neighbours.
  \item \textbf{Baselines at $B=128$}: with ResNet-18 they are a weak reference
        for the accuracy. Only FedAvg was run at $B=16$; SAGE and ESCS at
        $B=16$ would probably gain about as much.
  \item \textbf{Not converged}: in the last 50 rounds SAGE-smart still gains 2
        points, FedAvg 0.7 (FedAvg at $B=16$ is almost flat after round 175).
        Ranking at 250 rounds; with fewer than about 180 rounds FedAvg at
        $B=128$ would be ahead.
  \item \textbf{Depletion is permanent}: a device that reaches 0 never
        recharges. It drives the second half of the FedAvg runs (66 devices
        lost).
  \item \textbf{Leave-one-out does not see interactions}: removing the adaptive
        epochs also changes the batch, through the deadline.
  \item \textbf{The deadline} is computed on the baselines' nominal work ($E=5$,
        $B=128$) and is the main limit on the epochs of SAGE-smart.
  \item Phones, 4G and users as in the 12/10 report.
\end{itemize}

%======================================================================
\section{Next steps}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Close the accuracy gap with FedAvg at $B=16$ without losing
        the battery protection.} Two knobs, both outside the battery logic:
        a longer deadline (\texttt{smart\_deadline\_mult} 1.5 and 2, a value
        in the configuration) and no SAGE ramp (all 20 clients from round 1,
        a small switch). SAGE-smart datasheet, three $\beta$: 6--9 runs.
  \item \textbf{Two more seeds} (7, 13) for FedAvg ($B=16$ and 128), SAGE
        (paper), ESCS-SD and SAGE-smart, datasheet battery: 15 runs per seed,
        about 15--20 hours each.
  \item \textbf{Decide what to keep in SAGE-smart.} FedNova, the SoC estimate
        and the negotiation do not help in this scenario. Either the method is
        simplified (plain averaging; the broadcast already gives a fresh SoC),
        or they are kept as safety mechanisms and shown in a scenario where
        they matter: no broadcast and pauses between rounds (stale
        information), or low batteries (frequent refusals).
  \item \textbf{How to present the contribution}: against a well-tuned FedAvg,
        SAGE-smart trades about 5 accuracy points for a population that stays
        alive (8 instead of 67 depleted devices) and 37\,\% less energy over
        the run. Whether this trade is the thesis claim, or the target is to
        reduce the 5 points with step 1, is to be discussed.
\end{enumerate}
