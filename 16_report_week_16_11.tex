\section{Reaching 40\,\% accuracy}
\label{sec:thr}

40\,\% (the same definition as fig7 of \texttt{plot\_curves.py}) and read, at
that round: the energy drawn from the batteries since the start, the depleted
devices, the mean SoC of the devices still alive, and the devices below 20\,\%
of SoC (depleted included). Every value is the mean over 9 runs (3 $\beta$
$\times$ 3 seeds); all runs reach the threshold. FedAvg and FedProx run only
with the Ideal battery; SAGE with the Ideal battery is the version of the
paper, with Peukert and Datasheet it is the ablation ($a \cdot \mathrm{SoC} + b
\cdot D$). All baselines use $E = 5$, $B = 128$ and learning rate 0.01.

\begin{itemize}
  \item SAGE-smart reaches 40\,\% almost as soon as FedAvg in rounds (71
        against 66) and much sooner than SAGE and ESCS (75--121).
  \item It gets there with half the energy of FedAvg (320--333 Wh against
        676, $-52\,\%$) and about a third of SAGE and ESCS (867--1151 Wh).
  \item No device is depleted and practically none is below 20\,\% (0.1 on
        average, against 12.2 for FedAvg and 9--39 for SAGE and ESCS). The
        alive devices have on average 59\,\% of SoC, against 52\,\% for
        FedAvg and 45--51\,\% for SAGE and ESCS.
  \item In simulated time it takes 2.6 hours, against 7.1 for FedAvg and
        9--13 for SAGE and ESCS: with the adaptive $E$ the rounds are shorter.
  \item The depleted devices of FedAvg at the threshold all come from $\beta =
        0.1$ (10.7). At $\beta = 0.5$ and 1.0 FedAvg reaches 40\,\% before any
        device dies; there the difference is in energy ($-53\,\%$), SoC of the
        alive devices (+9--10 points) and devices below 20\,\%. After 250
        rounds FedAvg has depleted 58 devices, SAGE-smart 2--4.
  \item The battery model does not change the behaviour of SAGE-smart at the
        threshold: round 71 and 320--333 Wh with all three.
\end{itemize}

\section{To do now}
\label{sec:todo}

\begin{itemize}
  \item The baselines may be handicapped: run FedAvg, FedProx, SAGE and ESCS
        at $B = 16$, learning rate 0.01, 3 seeds $\times$ 3 $\beta$ (FedAvg at
        $B = 16$ exists for seed 2026).
  \item Control: FedAvg at $B = 128$ with learning rate 0.08 (and 0.04), i.e.\
        the learning rate scaled linearly with the batch.
  \item First and most urgent test: FedAvg at $B = 16$, learning rate 0.01,
        with a SoC filter as guard. If it matches our results in battery and
        accuracy, the method needs something more.
  \item In SAGE-smart the client almost always picks $B = 16$: the loop starts
        from the smallest $B$ and the highest $E$, and $B = 16$ is free (the
        throughput does not depend on $B$ and the batch overhead is
        negligible).
  \item Changes: latency and energy as a function of the batch, so that small
        batches pay for the lower parallelism; learning rate adapted to the
        batch.
  \item Selling points of the paper: (1) FL that does not deplete the phones
        at the same accuracy, with depleted devices and devices below 20\,\%
        as the main metrics; (2) late binding; (3) realistic SoC: current
        methods model the battery badly, while the real SoC is very uncertain.
  \item Present these points to Ale and Massimo.
\end{itemize}
